# Outline

`01-getting-started`
- Offer definition of "AI application development"
- Explain the kinds of people / backgrounds that benefit most. Contrast with poor-performing projects I've seen
    - Reference MIT study 95% of projects fail
- Discuss core topics

- Discuss what this is NOT good at:
    - Understanding core LLM mechanics
    - Training / finetuning models
    - Building recommedation or classifier systems
    - Multimodal-centric workflows 
    - Platform overengineering for billions of users (different from a system design course)
- What this course is GOOD at:
    - Giving exposure to key subsystems that nearly all AI systems require to perform well in the wild 
        - Data persistence
        - Resilience to upstream failures
        - Upload handling
        - Caching / cost reduction
        - Data cleaning for ingestion
    - Access to and understanding of a good reference architecture to take into your own work/projects
    - Sharing pitfalls from building a small platform from 0
    - Exposing you to basic frontend/backend architecture for chat-based workflows with good assumptions
    - Showing you a design for a minimal system that would not be a complete embarrassment at a major corporation
    - Presenting a nuanced view on agent versus non-agentic workflows

- Remind that this content is provisional and very current
